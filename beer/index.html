Joshua Chapter 3

Following the Ark

A Discussion Guide for Bible Study

FACILITATOR'S ANSWER KEY

I. Overall Theme of Joshua 3

The overarching theme of Joshua 3 is: Transitioning into God's Promise requires Consecration, Obedience, and a Singular Focus on God's Presence.

II. Question by Question Takeaways

Q1 (Departure from Shittim):

Key Takeaway: Beginning a new chapter with God requires separation and consecration. God demands purity and a clear break from past sins before moving into the inheritance he has prepared.

Q2 (Preparation/Officers):

Key Takeaway: God is a God of order and purpose. While miracles require faith, they often happen within an ordered framework. The "army of God" must be ready, organized, and prepared to move when God commands.

Q3 (Holiness/Distance):

Key Takeaway: The distance emphasized the absolute holiness of God and the sinfulness of humanity. The presence of God could not be approached casually without purification, teaching reverence and awe.

Q4 (Trust/Distance):

Key Takeaway: It teaches singular focus and blind obedience. Their trust was placed entirely on God's presence, not on their sight or human reasoning.

Q5 (Action/Overflowing Water):

Key Takeaway: Faith precedes the miracle. God required an act of terrifying obedience—taking the first step into the current—before His power was demonstrated.

Q6 (Stillness):

Key Takeaway: The priests had to stand still where God placed them. This teaches dependence—we rely on God to hold back the obstacles (the water), not on our strength to overcome them.

Q7 (Conclusion/Grace):

Key Takeaway: Christ became our "Ark"—His sacrifice fulfilled the need for separation, allowing us to "Draw near to find mercy" (Hebrews 4:16). The distance in Joshua 3 shows us what we deserved, making the intimacy we now have incredibly precious.

Personal Application:

Sample Answer/Focus: Drawing near in practice looks like intentionally dedicating time to prayer and Bible reading, or actively confessing known sin without fear, trusting that Christ's work has made the connection close. It means responding to obstacles (overflowing rivers) by stepping forward in faith rather than retreating in fear.

1. Setting the Scene: The Significance of Shittim (vs. 1-2)

Your notes highlight a key detail: Israel was moving away from Shittim, a place historically known for sin (Numbers 25).

Discussion Question 1 (Departure):

What does it signify that God had Israel's first official act under Joshua be a move away from a place known for sin? What does this tell us about beginning a new chapter with God?

Discussion Question 2 (Preparation):

What does the action of the officers going through the camp (showing the "army of God") suggest about God's plan? Why is organization necessary for a spiritual journey or "quest"?

2. The Tension of Distance: Separation vs. Nearness (vs. 3-4)

The mandate: follow the Ark (God's Presence), but maintain a significant distance—enough so it was the "only thing to focus on," requiring them to "Follow blindly."

Discussion Question 3 (Holiness):

The Ark represented holiness. Why was a physical "Distance for holiness" required in the Old Testament? What was the risk of approaching it "casually?"

Discussion Question 4 (Trust):

What does following the presence of God with a mandated distance teach us about the nature of faith and obedience?

3. Stepping into the Impossible (vs. 7-13)

The Jordan was overflowing, yet the priests had to step in *before* the water stopped. This required Joshua to be told, "I will be with you, as I was with Moses."

Discussion Question 5 (Action):

What is the significance of the priests having to step into the overflowing river *before* the way was clear? What "overflowing rivers" (impossible obstacles) do we face that require us to step in before seeing the solution?

Discussion Question 6 (Stillness):

God's power made the priests "Stand still in the water" on dry ground. Where is God asking us to stand still and trust Him to hold back the current in our lives?

4. The New Way: Grace and Drawing Near

The Old Testament required separation; the New Testament, through Christ, invites us to "Draw near to find mercy" (Hebrews 4:16). God allowing the Ark to move shows grace.

Discussion Question 7 (Conclusion):

How does understanding the necessary separation in Joshua 3 help you appreciate the freedom and privilege we have now, through Christ, to "draw near" to God?

Personal Application:

What does it look like in your life right now to draw near to God's presence, knowing that Jesus is the "new way?"
